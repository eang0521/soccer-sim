How does this work?
The pitch is five fixed zones, left to right: Attacking Third — Middle Third — Center Circle — Middle Third — Attacking Third. Each Attacking Third is one team's shooting zone and the other team's own defensive zone at the same time — whichever end it is depends on who's attacking through it. Ratings run 0–10, weighted like a 2d6−2 roll — most players cluster around 5, with 0 or 10 rare — so hitting "Randomize ratings" gives you a realistic-looking talent spread. Player 1 on each roster is the goalkeeper — only their Def rating matters while they're between the posts, but you can still give them an Off rating too, in case you (or "Auto-formation") decide they're better used elsewhere and move someone else into goal.

Who's involved. In a Middle Third or the Center Circle, each side puts forward one random player, weighted by position: the attacking side is weighted toward Forwards (Forward × 3, Midfielder × 2, Defender × 1), the defending side toward Defenders (Defender × 3, Midfielder × 2, Forward × 1) — so an out-of-position player can always get pulled into a contest, just less often. Reaching an Attacking Third works the same way for the entry duel, and once through, the shot itself uses that same weighted pick again — any position can end up taking the shot, just Forwards are still most likely to be the one there — against the defending goalkeeper.

The roll. Both players roll 1d20 + their rating (Off for the attacking player, Def for the defending player) and compare totals. Your formation (2-2-1, 2-1-2, 3-1-1, 1-3-1) sets how many players you have at each position, which shifts the odds of who gets picked. There are two rating bonuses: the goalkeeper's Def gets +50% (rounded down) on the shot, and a Midfielder's rating gets that same +50% boost whenever the ball is in the Center Circle — the one zone that's genuinely theirs, so stacking midfielders actually buys you something specific, not just a generic split between attack and defense. Higher total wins: in a Middle Third or Center Circle contest, winning advances the ball a zone, a tie leaves it contested in place, and losing is a turnover. In the Attacking Third, winning the entry duel puts the attacker in the box to face the keeper next; winning the shot duel is a goal — the ball resets to the Center Circle and the scoring team kicks off... to the team that just conceded. If a Defender wins the entry duel, they just win the ball back — it stays put in that same third, so the team that just turned it over now has to work it all the way back out themselves. Only a goalkeeper's save actually clears the ball out to the neighboring Middle Third. A tie at either step just means the attack stays alive and tries again next minute.

Extra time and penalties. If the checkbox is on and the match is level at full time, it goes to two 7-minute periods of extra time (with a halftime swap between them, and a fresh coin flip for who kicks off — same as regulation). Still level after that? It goes to a shootout. Kicking order for each team is all 6 players ranked by Off, highest first — goalkeepers included, so a goalkeeper with a big Off rating can end up taking an early penalty. Each shot is the kicker's Off +50% against the defending goalkeeper's plain Def — no bonus for the keeper, since penalties are normally hard to stop. The base round is the usual 5 kicks per side (ending early the moment a team can no longer mathematically catch up, same as a real shootout) — the 6th, lowest-Off player only steps up if it's still level after those 5. If it goes past that, both sides just cycle back through the same order, starting from the highest Off again, for sudden death.
